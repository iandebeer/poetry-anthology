Tao of the Dassie (Camps Bay)

The dassies of Bakoven sit in clusters, daring the sun's early rays
upon round rocks that rise like elephant trunks in the shallow tide —
the round rocks that the Twelve have granted them;

Unpretentious, stretched in the moment, where calmness is abundant,
and they need not steal each other's sun —
on the round rocks that the Twelve have granted them;

Bright emeralds of grass, pink figs, and yellow larkspurs,
that bloom in the sandy shelter —
from the round rocks that the Twelve have granted them;

And among the foam that drapes like cirrus clouds across the water,
float the sea bamboo like thousands of baying dassies —
gleaming bodies among the great round rocks that the Twelve have granted them;

Neither the black oystercatcher, shrill in flight,
nor the pounding procession of anxious sea-surfers, capturing fleeting moments,
disturb the slumbering satori in which they count the blessings of the Twelve.
