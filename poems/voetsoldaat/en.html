Confession of a Foot Soldier

Strange, the peace of Christmas Day;
strange, with the sound of "Silent Night"
upon lips that usually curse and berate
when bombs explode in a flaming hell.

But the very next morn, we struck again—
the peace shattered by the gunfire’s crack;
the calm and quiet of twenty-four hours
quickly devoured by blood and by flame.

Everywhere was hate, and everywhere was strife,
everywhere the scent of burning chalk,
with a reckless primal urge raging within me,
my mind dulls, as strength overcomes me:

Suddenly from a hut, a child bursts forth,
but he barely runs before my sights take hold;
and when his body trembles down in the dust,
from his small hands rolls a soccer ball.
