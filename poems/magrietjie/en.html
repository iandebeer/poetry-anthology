Song for a Daisy

This morning,
while a school bell rings somewhere in the distance for a short break,
I sprawled on a grass patch
nestled among clover, roundabouts, and teeter-totters,
on daisies that, like early spring cotton,
stretched wide and unblemished over the field,
with here and there a dot of yellow sorrel
drawing your gaze in the sunlight—

here where little feet and pushchair wheels tread on the flowers
and old folks stroll with their old dogs along the pathways,
I could draw the scent of damp earth and wet poplar leaves
deep into my lungs,
and in the umbrella mushrooms
that poke through the pine needles,
my soul rejoiced

like in a dream where I felt my limbs melt
beneath the morning sun and the sky’s blue,
and allowed this old earth to enfold me gently,
knowing it was right—
like the daisies,
I was here yesterday,
but now, I am gone.
